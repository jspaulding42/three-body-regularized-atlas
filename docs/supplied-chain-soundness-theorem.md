# Supplied-Chain Soundness Theorem: Specification and Gap Analysis

> **Historical/superseded note.** This gap analysis records an earlier checker
> stage and is retained for provenance; it is not the current theorem or
> normative certificate specification. See the
> [raw repeated planar continuation-chain theorem](raw-repeated-planar-continuation-chain-theorem.md)
> and [raw-v1 certificate specification](raw-v1-certificate-specification.md).

## Purpose

This document defines the first theorem the certificate checker should support.
It separates three statements that the previous proof package conflated:

1. a finite polynomial has a bounded equation defect;
2. an exact solution exists in a validated tube around that polynomial; and
3. the exact solution in that tube is the solution of a specified initial-value
   problem and is covered by an accepted chain of charts.

The current checker substantially supports the first statement.  It does not
yet establish the second or third.

## Current acceptance theorem

Let a **legacy accepted bundle** be a bundle for which
`verify_chart_certificates(...)` returns `certified=True`.  From the present
checker implementation one may conclude only the following syntactic and
finite-computation facts:

- every referenced chart has the expected finite array shapes and positive
  finite masses;
- its serialized polynomial coefficients satisfy the checker's finite
  recurrence and interval residual inequalities within certificate-supplied
  tolerances;
- an LC or KS chart satisfies the finite regularized constraints and projected
  residual checks implemented by its checker on the checked subdomain;
- every accepted transition has pointwise polynomial state and physical-time
  gaps within certificate-supplied tolerances;
- every accepted event polynomial has the checked sign/root pattern;
- chart identifiers and declared physical-time intervals satisfy the current
  chain grammar; and
- declared chart intervals syntactically cover the chain's declared target
  interval.

This is a **verified approximate-chain theorem**.  It does not imply that an
exact Newtonian solution exists in a tube around the polynomials, that the
chain corresponds to a requested initial condition, or that declared event and
physical-time metadata are generated by the checked chart polynomials.

## Desired mathematical theorem

The target theorem is:

> **Validated supplied-chain theorem.** Fix positive masses and a certified
> initial-state enclosure at physical time \(t_0\).  If a finite bundle of
> ordinary, LC, KS, and optional maximal-classical stop charts passes the
> validated checker, then there is a unique compatible exact solution of the
> Newtonian initial-value problem on the certified physical-time domain before
> the stop.  On every ordinary chart the exact state lies in the certified
> state tube.  On every regularized chart its lift lies in the certified lifted
> tube and its projection agrees with the same exact Newtonian branch on the
> punctured physical domain.  Accepted transitions identify overlapping
> enclosures of that same branch.  The union of the chart images covers the
> claimed physical-time interval.  If a total-stop chart is present, the
> maximal classical branch terminates at an independently certified total
> collision; no continuation through that event is claimed.

Acceptance must imply this theorem using only the explicit trusted kernel and
named mathematical lemmas below.  Constructor metadata and Python Boolean
properties are not hypotheses of the theorem.

## Required certificate extensions

### 1. Problem binding

Add a bundle-level problem record containing:

- a canonical mass vector;
- physical initial time;
- position and velocity intervals for all three bodies;
- dimension and coordinate convention;
- center-of-mass convention, if reduction is used; and
- an exact digest of the serialized problem data.

The checker must require every chart to use the same masses and dimension.  The
first chart must enclose the problem's initial state at the initial time.  A
chain that is not bound to a problem may still be an approximate-chain
certificate, but not an IVP solution certificate.

### 2. A-posteriori local existence certificate

For an ordinary chart with approximate state \(p(t)\), define its defect

\[
d(t)=p'(t)-F(p(t)).
\]

A validated tube certificate must provide checker-recomputable bounds
\(\|d\|\leq\delta\), \(\|DF\|\leq L\) on a collision-free tube of radius
\(R\), and initial mismatch \(\varepsilon\).  For a chart of physical width
\(h\), the checker may use the standard bound

\[
E(h)=e^{Lh}\varepsilon+
\begin{cases}
\delta (e^{Lh}-1)/L,&L>0,\\
\delta h,&L=0.
\end{cases}
\]

It must additionally verify the self-consistency inequality \(E(h)\leq R\)
and that the entire radius-\(R\) tube stays inside the collision-free domain.
Directed or rational interval arithmetic must be used for the upper bound.

An equivalent radii-polynomial, Picard, or Newton-Kantorovich certificate is
acceptable if its hypotheses and conclusion are explicit and independently
checked.

LC and KS charts require the analogous theorem for their regularized lifted
vector fields, followed by a checked projection bound.  Merely bounding the
projected polynomial residual is not a substitute for lifted existence.

### 3. Non-vacuous tolerance policy

Current chart and transition tolerances are arbitrary nonnegative finite
numbers.  The validated checker must derive admissible tolerances from tube
radii and overlap inequalities.  A large user-supplied tolerance cannot make a
false transition or residual certificate true.

### 4. Physical-time image

For ordinary charts, the absolute relation between parameter and physical time
must be bound, not only the interval widths.  For LC and KS charts the checker
must validate:

- the polynomial/interval enclosure for \(t(s)\);
- the sign of \(dt/ds\) on each nondegenerate segment;
- monotonicity and orientation;
- endpoint image enclosures; and
- that the declared physical interval is contained in the proven image, rather
  than merely containing sampled or polynomial values.

The chain coverage proof must use these proven images instead of free metadata.

### 5. Enclosure transitions

A transition must show that the source and target **solution enclosures**
overlap at a common physical-time enclosure and select the same unique local
solution.  Pointwise polynomial values within arbitrary tolerances are
insufficient.  Transition order must agree with chain order, and the transition
time must lie in the proven overlap of adjacent physical-time images.

### 6. Identity and content binding

Require global uniqueness of chart IDs, certificate IDs, transition IDs, event
IDs, chain IDs, and union IDs.  Checker results must be bound to a canonical
digest of the exact serialized object they checked.  A certified result for one
object must not satisfy a different object with a colliding identifier.

### 7. Event provenance

An event certificate must reference a checked chart and a named scalar function
derived by the checker from that chart.  Supplied polynomial coefficients and a
whitelisted `coefficient_source` string do not establish that the isolated root
is an event of the trajectory.

### 8. Stop semantics

A total-collision stop requires two logically separate certificates:

1. a local analytic enclosure/projection theorem for the supplied stop chart;
2. an event theorem proving that all three physical pair distances approach
   zero at the endpoint and remain positive on the punctured incoming domain.

Supplied majorant constants must either be recomputed as bounds on the actual
analytic operator or be explicit mathematical assumptions.  Algebraically
checking a contraction inequality over unverified constants is conditional
evidence only.

## Trusted kernel

The intended trusted computing base is:

- parsing of a versioned canonical certificate format;
- exact integer/rational arithmetic and directed elementary-function bounds;
- interval polynomial evaluation;
- explicit formulas for Newton, LC, and KS vector fields and projections;
- finite recurrence, residual, derivative, tube, and overlap checks;
- a small collection of proved a-posteriori ODE lemmas; and
- finite chain induction.

NumPy floating-point evaluation, constructor classes, sampled residuals,
`source` strings, and precomputed `certified` Boolean fields are not part of the
trusted theorem boundary.

## Proof structure

Once the required certificates exist, the proof is finite induction.

1. The problem-binding check and first local-existence certificate give the
   unique exact lifted or physical solution in the first tube.
2. A transition enclosure plus local uniqueness identifies the next chart's
   exact solution with the previous one on their overlap.
3. Induction identifies every accepted chart with one maximal branch.
4. Proven physical-time images and checked overlap order show that their union
   covers the target interval without gaps.
5. Projection lemmas turn accepted LC/KS lifted branches into the same
   Newtonian branch away from the collision divisor and justify the selected
   regularized binary continuation.
6. If present, an independently validated total-collision event terminates the
   maximal classical conclusion at its endpoint.

This argument proves soundness for a supplied finite chain.  It says nothing
about whether a constructor can find such a chain for every input.

## Immediate implementation order

1. Add canonical content digests and reject duplicate identities. **Duplicate
   chart and certificate identities are now rejected; canonical content
   digests remain to be added.**
2. Add a problem-binding certificate and first-chart initial-state check.
   **`InitialValueProblemBindingCertificate` and
   `check_initial_value_problem_binding(...)` now implement the finite
   binary-float binding check as a standalone prerequisite.  Bundle-level
   mandatory integration awaits the tube certificate.**
3. Add an ordinary-chart a-posteriori tube certificate and checker.
   **`OrdinaryAposterioriTubeCertificate` and
   `check_ordinary_aposteriori_tube(...)` now recompute the direct interval
   Newton defect, a collision-free tube floor, a conservative Newton-field
   Lipschitz bound, and the Grönwall self-consistency inequality.  The next
   step is to bind its anchor error to an accepted IVP binding inside one
   aggregate validated-bundle result. `check_validated_ordinary_ivp_chart(...)`
   now performs that binding and rejects cases where individually accepted
   IVP/tube records do not cover the actual polynomial-to-IVP mismatch.  This
   establishes the first local exact-solution enclosure primitive; it is not
   yet integrated into multi-chart bundle acceptance.**
4. Make chart-chain coverage consume proven physical-time images.
5. Replace point transition checks with enclosure-overlap checks.
   **The ordinary-to-ordinary induction step is now implemented by
   `OrdinaryEnclosureTransitionCertificate` and
   `check_ordinary_enclosure_transition(...)`.  It requires a common physical
   handoff time and proves that the target tube's anchor error covers the sum
   of the source tube's proven error and the polynomial state gap.  Tests reject
   arbitrary pointwise tolerance handoffs whose target tube is too small.
   `ValidatedOrdinaryIVPChainCertificate` and
   `check_validated_ordinary_ivp_chain(...)` now iterate this implication,
   require a forward nondecreasing frontier without physical-time gaps, bind
   the first chart to the IVP, and reject a requested target interval beyond
   the proven union.  Proof-grade directed transcendental rounding and
   canonical bundle serialization remain to be completed.**
6. Extend the tube theorem to planar LC, then spatial KS.
7. Bind event polynomials to checked chart expressions.
8. Keep Fuchsian total-stop acceptance conditional until its analytic operator
   majorants are independently derived.

## Publication boundary

Until the validated supplied-chain theorem is implemented, public descriptions
should use “independently checked finite polynomial residual certificates” and
not “verified trajectory enclosure” or “proof-certified general closed form.”
After implementation, the supplied-chain soundness theorem can stand as an
independent result even if global certificate-construction completeness remains
open.
