# Research Direction Audit

## Decision

Preserve the project, but suspend the claim that it already proves a general
closed solution of the three-body problem.

The mathematically promising object is the certificate language for a locally
finite atlas of ordinary Taylor, Levi-Civita, Kustaanheimo-Stiefel, and
total-collision stop charts.  The current repository provides substantial
constructors and checkers for *supplied* local charts and finite chart chains.
It does not yet prove or implement a total constructor that finds such a chain
for every advertised exact or computable point input.

This document is the working research baseline.  A Boolean called
`proof_certified` in the current Python model is not, by itself, evidence that
the corresponding mathematical proposition has been proved.

## Strongest defensible result today

The strongest fully composed result is now a **validated local planar binary-
collision passage for a supplied exact lifted IVP**. The checker can anchor a
unique LC solution at an exact constrained collision, prove that collision is
isolated, verify the punctured LC/Newton projection algebra, and enclose the
same solution in weighted ordinary Newton tubes on both physical sides. The
standalone witness is generated by
`scripts/certify_planar_binary_collision_passage.py`; its theorem and trusted
kernel are stated in `docs/validated-planar-binary-collision-passage.md` and
`docs/validated-planar-lc-tube-theorem.md`.

Beyond that local theorem, the repository implements an experimental
certificate language and checker for supplied finite chains of regularized
three-body charts. It contains:

- ordinary Taylor charts on collision-free domains;
- planar Levi-Civita charts for separated binary encounters;
- local spatial KS charts, including gauge and projection checks;
- supplied Fuchsian/Puiseux-log total-collision stop charts;
- interval residual, tail, transition, and invariant checks; and
- finite-chain and finite-prefix aggregation.

Subject to an audit of the checker kernel, this supports a **soundness theorem
for accepted supplied certificates**.  It does not yet support completeness of
certificate construction for arbitrary initial data.

## Decisive implementation mismatches

### The fair search is not a search

`certify_finite_target_certificate_search_completeness(...)` describes an
enumeration of rational candidate certificates, but does not construct or run
that enumeration.  Its fairness and termination fields inherit the truth value
of the abstract finite-target theorem.

Likewise,
`derive_computable_atlas_certificate_enumeration_from_pointwise_theorem(...)`
sets the enumeration and query-termination fields from theorem metadata.  It
does not supply an encoding theorem, candidate generator, verifier dovetail, or
termination proof.

A genuine dovetail argument requires all of the following:

1. a recursively enumerable finite certificate syntax;
2. a decidable checker for each finite candidate;
3. a robustness theorem showing that every required trajectory admits an
   accepted finitely encoded witness, normally with strict rational margins;
4. effective access to the initial data and every required chart parameter;
5. special treatment of equality strata and total-collision data where strict
   margins may disappear.

None of these may be inferred merely from non-effective certificate existence.

### The advertised input model is not implemented

The theorem labels include exact and computable point inputs.  The practical
input-domain constructor immediately coerces masses, positions, and velocities
to binary64 floats.  There is no computable-real oracle, exact algebraic input
type, or precision-refinement protocol.  The implemented constructor therefore
has floating-point seed scope, with interval wrapping, rather than arbitrary
computable-real scope.

### Supplied-witness soundness is not constructor completeness

Several routes accept a caller-supplied `ValidatedAtlasSolution`, branch tree,
event ordering, Fuchsian entry, or majorant.  Checking these objects can be
valuable and rigorous.  It does not show that the repository can find them for
an arbitrary input.

## Central mathematical frontier

The dominant gap is the implication

> arbitrary positive-mass three-body total-collision germ
> -> finite, effectively encodable, verifier-accepted generalized-Fuchsian
> stop data.

The current checker establishes useful implications after suitable entry rows,
selector information, isolation data, and majorant constants are supplied.
The universal existence and effective-construction bridge is not established.
This bridge combines deep collision asymptotics, reduced hyperbolicity,
resonances, orientation/gauge recovery, analytic remainder bounds, and
computability.  It should be treated as a research theorem, not as an
implementation detail.

## Revised research program

### Milestone 1: supplied-chain soundness theorem

Write and prove a theorem of the following form:

> If a finite serialized chain of ordinary, LC, KS, and total-stop chart
> certificates is accepted by a specified small checker, then its projection
> encloses a maximal classical Newtonian three-body trajectory on the claimed
> time domain, with the stated target or total-collision-stop outcome.

Required outputs:

- a minimal serialized certificate grammar;
- a small checker whose trusted operations are explicit;
- a conventional proof by induction over chart transitions;
- adversarial mutation tests; and
- a second checker implementation or formalized checker semantics.

This result is useful without completeness and is the best near-term candidate
for a defensible computer-assisted mathematics contribution.

### Milestone 2: local chart theorems

Extract paper-quality soundness theorems for ordinary Taylor, planar LC, and
spatial KS charts.  State exact domains, gauge conditions, time
reparameterizations, projection identities, residual norms, and handoff
conditions.  Replace implementation prose with equations and citations.

### Milestone 3: a real effective constructor on a restricted domain

Choose an explicit input class, initially rational binary64 seeds with rational
interval enclosures and a finite target known to precede total collision.
Implement the candidate enumeration or an adaptive validated constructor.
Record success, typed obstruction, and resource exhaustion separately.  Do not
claim termination until it is proved.

### Milestone 4: total-collision research theorem

Isolate the arbitrary-germ-to-stop-data bridge into a standalone mathematical
paper.  It must be allowed to fail without invalidating Milestones 1-3.  If it
cannot be proved effectively, retain total-collision charts as supplied
certificates and state the global result conditionally or only before first
total collision.

### Milestone 5: completeness

Only after Milestones 1-4 should the project revisit finite-target completeness,
computable enumeration, and the open-time atlas.  Interval-box uniformity is a
later and strictly stronger theorem.

## Go/no-go tests

Continue toward the broad theorem only if the project can establish:

- robust finite encodings for every local certificate used in the existence
  proof;
- checker decidability independent of constructor success;
- a noncircular effectiveness proof for the point-input search;
- a rigorous spatial KS isolation/handoff theorem;
- a rigorous arbitrary-total-collision entry theorem or an explicitly narrower
  pre-total-collision scope; and
- external review of the two hardest analytic bridges.

If those fail, retain the project as a supplied-certificate checker and
validated close-encounter solver.  That remains potentially publishable and
scientifically useful.

## Immediate next task

Define the trusted kernel for the supplied-chain soundness theorem.  Inventory
which fields of the existing certificate classes are recomputed by the checker,
which are trusted, and which are unused metadata.  The resulting boundary will
determine whether the current checker can support a mathematical soundness
claim or needs to be reduced first.
