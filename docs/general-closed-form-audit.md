# General Closed-Form Audit

## Target

The active target is a general closed-form solution to the Newtonian three-body problem, with proof. To count as complete, a candidate must handle arbitrary positive masses and arbitrary non-collision initial position/velocity data, then return body positions for time `t` and prove that those positions satisfy Newton's equations on the full interval claimed.

## Current Mathematical Constraints

The phrase "closed form" needs a precise function class before it can be proved or disproved. Under the classical algebraic-integral interpretation, the target is blocked by known nonintegrability results:

- Bruns' theorem says the classical ten integrals are the only algebraically independent integrals for the three-body problem: center-of-mass position, center-of-mass velocity, angular momentum, and energy. Source: https://scienceworld.wolfram.com/physics/BrunsTheorem.html
- Modern nonintegrability results continue this obstruction in analytic/meromorphic settings. For example, Yagasaki summarizes Poincare's restricted-three-body result as nonexistence of an additional real-analytic first integral independent of the Hamiltonian and then proves broader restricted-problem nonintegrability. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04925
- Sundman's theorem gives an exact analytic route by globally convergent series, under regularization and collision-continuation conventions, but it is an infinite series rather than a finite closed form. Sundman's Acta Mathematica memoir states that the final series in powers of the transformed variable converges and represents the motion for all times under the stated collision continuation. Source: https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/117/5229-11511_2006_Article_BF02422379.pdf

This split is now executable in `three_body_symmetry/closed_form.py`. `certify_closed_form_target("closed form")` requires an explicit allowed function class unless a typed `ClosedFormFunctionClassWitnessCertificate` defines the representation, evaluation, convergence, and Newton-equation verification semantics; `certify_closed_form_target("finite algebraic first integrals")` returns a certified obstruction branch; and `certify_closed_form_target("sundman global series")` imports missing compact-Sundman global proof obligations when a witness is supplied. The finite-integral obstruction branch now also carries internal evidence from `three_body_symmetry/obstructions.py`: two equal-mass zero-velocity states have matching center of mass, linear momentum, angular momentum, and energy within tolerance, while their Newtonian acceleration signatures are separated. The stricter `certify_general_closed_form_solution_target(...)` then adds the full theorem scope: arbitrary positive masses, arbitrary non-collision initial data, all real target times, a certified lift/construct/project/verify pipeline, compact-Sundman global series certification, collision continuation, and Newton-equation verification on the full claimed interval. Each top-level requirement now reports required evidence, observed witness flags, and compact-Sundman blocker ids where applicable; the arbitrary-data/all-time part can be supplied as either the aggregate `GeneralSolutionScopeWitnessCertificate` or the granular `GeneralSolutionTheoremScopeWitnessCertificate` with separate mass-domain, noncollision-domain, compact-time, Sundman-targeting, lift/construction/projection, and Newton-residual obligations, collision continuation can be supplied as a typed `CollisionContinuationWitnessCertificate` with separate binary-continuation, nonzero-angular triple-exclusion, and zero-angular triple-convention obligations, and the whole Sundman route can now be composed as a typed `SundmanGeneralSolutionTheoremWitnessCertificate` that joins recurrence closure, collision continuation, and theorem-scope evidence. This keeps the harness from treating a vague closed-form request, a finite closed-form no-go theorem, a local target-time enclosure, or a scoped series witness as if any one of them were already the requested general solution.

9. `three_body_symmetry/sundman.py` implements a Sundman-time lift. It introduces a fictitious variable `s` with

```text
dt/ds = g(q) = product over i < j of |q_i - q_j|
dq_i/ds = g(q) v_i
dv_i/ds = g(q) a_i(q)
```

For `g(q) > 0`, the projected curve satisfies the original Newtonian equations by the chain rule:

```text
dq_i/dt = (dq_i/ds)/(dt/ds) = v_i
dv_i/dt = (dv_i/ds)/(dt/ds) = a_i(q)
```

As any pair distance tends to zero while the other distances stay nonzero, `g(q)` tends to zero. Physical time therefore advances more slowly in `s`, which is the basic regularizing mechanism needed for a Sundman-style global series. The Sundman layer now also has outward-rounded interval coefficient recurrences for `g(q)`, `q(s)`, `v(s)`, and `t(s)`, including interval initial data that certify non-collision. It can now build an explicit interval residual certificate for the lifted equations, checking coefficient by coefficient that `dq/ds - g v`, `dv/ds - g a(q)`, and `dt/ds - g` all enclose zero. Fixed-`s` and physical-time-target interval continuations attach these residual certificates to every accepted chart, including the final target-containing chart. In Cauchy mode, accepted full Sundman interval steps now inflate their endpoint state and physical-time intervals by the local Cauchy tail before seeding the next chart. It can propagate those interval state sets through multiple fixed-`s` Sundman charts while carrying an accumulated interval for physical time; each accepted interval step now carries a positive enclosure for `dt/ds`, and steps without certified time monotonicity are rejected. Sundman charts can now certify that a requested positive or negative physical-time target is bracketed inside an `s` interval by proving `dt/ds > 0` over that interval and enclosing the target between interval endpoint times; this has been lifted from a single chart to multi-chart interval continuation. If a proposed full step would contain the target but the target bracket is not certified, the interval target continuation now halves the proposed `s` step and retries before accepting any full step. The continuation result objects now separate structural certification from proof certification: `certified` covers monotone Sundman time and lifted-equation residuals, while `proof_certified` also requires Cauchy tail certificates on every full step and on the final target chart when there is one. They also expose the triple-collision obstruction status explicitly, so zero-angular-momentum data remains `undecided` rather than being confused with a proved exclusion.
10. `three_body_symmetry/levi_civita.py` implements the planar isolated-binary collision chart. In relative complex coordinates,

```text
q = z^2
dt/ds = |z|^2
z'' = (h / 2) z
```

where `h` is the Kepler energy. The singular physical acceleration is recovered after projection for `z != 0`, while the lifted `z` equation remains finite at `z = 0`. This is the local collision mechanism needed before a full three-body binary-collision chart can be assembled.
11. `three_body_symmetry/binary_chart.py` embeds the Levi-Civita relative coordinate in a full planar three-body chart for a selected pair. r_ddot = -M r / |r|^3 + p(z, y)


`r_X=max_K r^X_K`. The new regression checks this direct composition from
primitive Cauchy data to all-future budget, including variable actual chart
counts below the per-shell envelope. This removes the need to separately
postulate shell budgets once the primitive dynamical estimates are proved.

The same theorem now explains where finite compact-prefix event counts come
from when local isolation is uniform. A separated binary Levi-Civita event has
`z(s)=zeta(0)s+O(s^2)` with `zeta(0) != 0`, so the binary collision is an
isolated zero of the lifted pair coordinate. An identity-selector total
collision has `q_i=tau^2S_i(tau)` with `S_i(0)-S_j(0) != 0`, so total
collision is isolated at `tau=0` inside the Fuchsian-log chart. If the induced
compact-time isolation radius in shell `S_n` is at least `alpha Delta_n`, then
a packing argument gives `E_n <= floor(1/alpha)+2`, and any compact prefix with
a positive minimum isolation radius has finitely many events. The remaining
dynamical problem is now sharper: prove such uniform isolation radii and shell
budget envelopes from arbitrary-data motion, or classify the exceptional
nongeometric/finite-accumulation cases.

The shell chart-count input is now derived from that same event isolation
constant. If `M_*=floor(1/alpha)+2`, then each shell has at most `M_*`
separated-binary charts, at most `M_*` automatic total-collision charts, and
at most `M_*+1` ordinary gap atlases, because the complement of `E_n` disjoint
event neighborhoods has at most `E_n+1` connected components. The new
regression feeds those derived counts into the primitive Cauchy all-future
budget formula. Thus the count side of the primitive recurrence is no longer
an independent assumption once uniform shell isolation is proved; the still
open dynamical input is the isolation constant itself and the Cauchy
majorant/step-ratio estimates.

The ordinary-gap Cauchy input is now proved in the uniformly noncollision
state-envelope regime. If every future ordinary gap chart has pair separation
at least `d_*>0`, pair diameter at most `D_*`, and speed at most `V_*`, then the
Sundman Cauchy state-envelope lemma gives a positive uniform radius floor
`R_*` and finite component majorants depending only on
`d_*`, `D_*`, `V_*`, total mass, and fixed projection/time-change factors.
Choosing chart steps with `h <= sigma R_*` gives fixed Cauchy fraction
`sigma<1`, `Lambda=1`, and retained-order growth produces the ordinary local
ratio `r_O=sigma^d<1`. The new regression checks the explicit `R_*` formula,
the `Lambda=1` ordinary primitive inputs, and the resulting all-future ordinary
gap tail bound. This closes the ordinary-gap primitive input for uniformly
separated bounded-speed regimes, but not for unscaled escape or for binary and
total-collision chart families.

The finite-accumulation exception is now sharpened further. On any compact
prefix away from the endpoint, if infinitely many verified events occur, the
certified local isolation radii must have infimum zero. For separated binary
events the regularized speed does not vanish, because `|zeta(0)|^2=M/2`, so
degeneration must come from shrinking third-body/noncolliding-pair separation,
loss of a uniform branch split, or a collapsing analytic chart radius. For
identity-selector total collisions, accumulation forces either collision-free
shape separation, the Fuchsian-log radius, or the finite-energy central-target
hypotheses to degenerate. Thus finite-prefix accumulation is no longer an
unstructured extra case; it is a signal that the branch is leaving the verified
geometric-isolation regime and must be handled by a separate degenerate-regime
classification.

The separated-binary side has an even sharper finite-time classification. If
binary collision times `c_n` accumulate at a finite physical time `T`, then one
binary pair repeats on an infinite subsequence. Continuity gives that pair
collision at `T`. If the third body remains separated at `T`, the local
Levi-Civita theorem supplies a punctured neighborhood in which that pair has no
other collision, contradicting `c_n -> T`. Therefore finite-time accumulation of
binary events is possible only as total collision. This removes "separated
binary finite accumulation" as a separate global regime; it must be handled by
the total-collision classification/continuation branch.

The nonzero-angular compact branch now inherits a finite binary-event theorem.
On a compact physical-time interval, infinitely many binary collision events
would have an accumulation point; the preceding paragraph routes that
accumulation to total collision. The full nonzero-angular theorem excludes
total collision for classical three-body motion with conserved nonzero centered
angular momentum. Therefore nonzero-angular compact intervals have only
finitely many binary collision events, and their singular atlas content is a
finite separated-binary Levi-Civita list plus ordinary Taylor charts. Infinite
binary-event recurrences remain necessary only for zero-angular or endpoint
accumulation regimes where this angular obstruction does not apply.

That consequence is now stated as a compact finite-atlas theorem. For a compact
nonzero-angular branch whose binary events are all separated-third-body
Levi-Civita events, total collision is excluded, binary events are finite, and
the complement is a finite union of collision-free compact intervals. Ordinary
Taylor covers on the complement plus the finite binary chart list give one
finite lift/construct/project/verify atlas, with finite value, first-jet,
lifted-residual, and projected-residual budgets obtained by summing the
ordinary and binary chart budgets.

The nonzero-angular compact finite-atlas theorem now composes with the
two-ended prescribed-scattering endpoint theorem. If the past and future
hyperbolic scattering endpoint charts have matching handoff states and the
compact connecting branch has conserved nonzero centered angular momentum with
only separated-third-body binary events, then the middle atlas is no longer an
extra hypothesis: it is the finite ordinary-plus-Levi-Civita atlas supplied by
the nonzero-angular compact theorem. The endpoint dyadic geometric sums are
unchanged, and the finite middle contributes only the summed ordinary/binary
value, first-jet, lifted-residual, and projected-residual budgets. No
identity-selector total-collision chart appears in this subcase, because total
collision is excluded before composition. This closes the all-real
prescribed-scattering branch with a nonzero-angular compact middle; it still
does not prove arbitrary-data scattering classification, a global scattering
map, or existence of such connecting branches for arbitrary prescribed
endpoints.
