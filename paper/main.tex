\begin{abstract}
We describe a small computer-assisted certificate for continuing one specified
planar Newtonian three-body trajectory through an isolated binary collision.
The construction combines an analytic Levi--Civita (LC) initial-value problem
across collision, an exact collision anchor, interval residual and Jacobian
bounds, exact symbolic checks of the punctured LC--Newton projection, and
anisotropic a posteriori tubes for ordinary Newtonian solutions on both sides.
Acceptance implies, subject to the stated outward-rounding hypothesis, that
the two punctured Newtonian branches are projections of the same unique lifted
LC solution and are correctly ordered in physical time.  A reproducible
example with masses $(1,1,1.2)$ is included in the accompanying open-source
implementation.  The result is deliberately local and planar: it is neither
a general solution of the three-body problem nor a global atlas-completeness
theorem.  The main contribution is a falsifiable certificate architecture
which makes branch identity at collision, often left implicit in numerical
continuation, an explicit checked obligation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Binary collision is a coordinate singularity of the Newtonian equations but
can be regularized in the planar problem by the classical Levi--Civita
transformation~\cite{LeviCivita1920,SiegelMoser1995,Montgomery2024}.
Analytic regularization alone, however, does not certify that
a computed polynomial describes an exact orbit, that its two physical
projections belong to one lifted initial-value problem, or that all required
force denominators remain separated.  Those points matter when a numerical
orbit is offered as evidence for a collision passage.

This paper isolates these obligations and turns them into a checkable local
certificate.  A serialized polynomial approximates a fourteen-dimensional
lifted LC system.  Interval evaluation bounds its differential defect and the
Jacobian of the regularized vector field on a proposed tube.  An exact,
zero-error anchor enforces collision and the pair-energy constraint.  On two
punctured endpoints the complete LC enclosure is projected into physical
coordinates and contained in independently validated Newtonian tubes.  The
same-IVP statement is therefore obtained by construction, not inferred from
overlapping floating-point boxes.

The implementation also checks the algebraic LC--Newton identities separately
with exact symbolic arithmetic.  Its example is intentionally modest: it
establishes one explicit local passage and provides a basis for larger
libraries of supplied, independently checkable collision charts.

\paragraph{Scope.}
No claim is made here of a closed form for arbitrary three-body initial data,
of continuation through total collision, of a spatial Kustaanheimo--Stiefel
theorem, or of termination and completeness of a global regularized atlas.
Those statements require substantially different arguments.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
The present construction draws on classical Levi--Civita regularization
\cite{LeviCivita1920,SiegelMoser1995,Montgomery2024} and established methods
for validated integration and interval enclosures
\cite{Lohner1987,NedialkovJacksonCorliss1999,MooreKearfottCloud2009}.
Capi\'nski, Kepley, and Mireles James proved transverse collision and
near-collision results by computer-assisted methods in the circular restricted
three-body problem~\cite{CapinskiKepleyMirelesJames2023}.  Their results employ
a different dynamical model and establish substantially stronger orbit-level
conclusions.  By contrast, the present artifact treats one local passage in
the full planar three-body model and remains conditional on its stated
interval-arithmetic trusted kernel.  This comparison is intended to locate the
work methodologically, not to assert novelty or priority.

\section{Planar three-body and lifted LC equations}

Let $m_0,m_1,m_2>0$ and choose a collision pair $(i,j)$, leaving body $k$.
Write $M=m_i+m_j$, $\alpha=m_j/M$, and $\beta=m_i/M$.  In the lifted state
\[
 X=(z,w,h,R,U,y,V,t)\in\R^{14},
\]
the entries $z,w,R,U,y,V$ are planar vectors and $h,t$ are scalars.  Define
\[
 Q(z)=(z_1^2-z_2^2,2z_1z_2),\qquad \rho=|z|^2,
\]
and
\[
 L(z)=2\begin{pmatrix}z_1&-z_2\\z_2&z_1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The selected relative position is $q=Q(z)$ and physical time satisfies
$dt/ds=\rho$.  Put
\[
 d_i=y+\alpha Q(z),\qquad d_j=y-\beta Q(z),\qquad
 f_i=\frac{d_i}{|d_i|^3},\quad f_j=\frac{d_j}{|d_j|^3},
\]
and set
\[
 B=\frac{m_k}{M}(m_i f_i+m_j f_j),\quad
 A_k=-m_i f_i-m_j f_j,
\]
\[
 Y=A_k-B,\qquad P=m_k(f_j-f_i).
\]
The regularized field used by the checker is
\begin{align}
 z'&=w, &
 w'&=\tfrac12hz+\tfrac14\rho L(z)^TP,\nonumber\\
 h'&=(L(z)w)\mathbin{\cdot}P, &
 R'&=\rho U, & U'&=\rho B,\label{eq:lcfield}\\
 y'&=\rho V, & V'&=\rho Y, & t'&=\rho.\nonumber
\end{align}
There is no division by $z$ or $\rho$.  Thus the field is analytic at the
selected binary collision whenever $d_i$ and $d_j$ stay nonzero.

The physical pair velocity on a punctured LC interval is
\[
 v=\frac{L(z)w}{\rho}.
\]
The remaining lifted variables reconstruct the three physical positions and
velocities through the pair center of mass and third-body offset.  The
implementation uses the same serialized masses in both coordinate systems.

\section{A posteriori tube validation}

Let $F$ denote either the lifted field~\eqref{eq:lcfield} or the first-order
ordinary Newtonian field, and let $\bar X$ be a polynomial on an interval of
half-width $\ell$.  The validation follows the standard a posteriori and
interval-enclosure viewpoint for ordinary differential equations
\cite{Lohner1987,NedialkovJacksonCorliss1999,MooreKearfottCloud2009}.
Suppose interval arithmetic proves, uniformly on the
declared tube,
\[
 \norm{\bar X'-F(\bar X)}_\infty\leq\delta,
 \qquad \norm{DF}_\infty\leq K,
\]
and the exact anchor differs from the polynomial anchor by at most
$\varepsilon$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 E=e^{K\ell}\varepsilon+\delta\frac{e^{K\ell}-1}{K},
 \label{eq:gronwall}
\end{equation}
with the continuous interpretation $E=\varepsilon+\delta\ell$ when $K=0$.

\begin{lemma}[A posteriori tube lemma]\label{lem:tube}
If every denominator of $F$ is bounded away from zero on a radius-$r$ tube
around $\bar X$ and $E<r$, then the exact anchored IVP has a unique solution
throughout the certified interval and
$\norm{X-\bar X}_\infty\leq E$ there.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Local existence and uniqueness hold on the nonsingular tube.  Subtracting the
integral equations for $X$ and $\bar X$ gives
\[
 e(s)\leq \varepsilon+\delta|s-a|+
 K\int_a^s e(u)\,du.
\]
Gr\"onwall's inequality yields~\eqref{eq:gronwall}.  If the solution first
left the radius-$r$ tube, the same strict estimate at that first-exit time
would give $e<r$, a contradiction.  The argument applies on either side of
the anchor and uniqueness joins the two solutions.
\end{proof}

For the LC tube, interval forward automatic differentiation evaluates both
$F$ and $DF$, and separately establishes positive lower bounds for
$|d_i|$ and $|d_j|$.  Hence no lower bound for $\rho$ is needed to validate
the lifted solution across $z=0$.

\subsection{Weighted ordinary tubes}

Projection near collision produces errors of different scales in position
and velocity.  A single state radius may therefore make the position domain
spuriously intersect collision.  For position and velocity radii $R_q,R_v$,
the checker instead uses
\[
 \|(\Delta q,\Delta v)\|_W=
 \max\{\|\Delta q\|_\infty/R_q,\|\Delta v\|_\infty/R_v\}.
\]
If $A$ bounds the infinity norm of the acceleration Jacobian on the position
tube, then
\[
 L_W=\max\{R_v/R_q,A R_q/R_v\}.
\]
With block defects $\delta_q,\delta_v$ and anchor errors
$\varepsilon_q,\varepsilon_v$, define
\[
 \eta_0=\max\{\varepsilon_q/R_q,\varepsilon_v/R_v\},\qquad
 \gamma=\max\{\delta_q/R_q,\delta_v/R_v\}.
\]
Acceptance requires
\[
 E_W=e^{L_W\ell}\eta_0+
 \gamma\frac{e^{L_W\ell}-1}{L_W}<1.
\]
The resulting physical error bounds are $R_qE_W$ and $R_vE_W$.  This is
Lemma~\ref{lem:tube} after a diagonal change of norm.

\section{Collision anchoring and projection}

Define the pair-energy constraint
\begin{equation}
 C=2|w|^2-M-\rho h.\label{eq:constraint}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Constraint invariance]\label{lem:constraint}
Every solution of~\eqref{eq:lcfield} satisfies $C'=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\rho'=2z\cdot w$, direct substitution gives
\[
 C'=4w\cdot w'-\rho'h-\rho h'
 =2h z\cdot w+\rho(Lw)\cdot P
 -2h z\cdot w-\rho(Lw)\cdot P=0.
\]
\end{proof}

The certificate evaluates the serialized polynomial at the collision anchor
using exact rational arithmetic over its binary floating-point coefficients.
It requires zero anchor uncertainty and
\begin{equation}
 z(0)=0,\qquad 2|w(0)|^2=M.\label{eq:anchor}
\end{equation}
Thus $C=0$ for the selected exact IVP.  Because $M>0$, $w(0)\ne0$; hence
$z$ has a simple isolated zero.  Moreover
\[
 t(s)-t(0)=\int_0^s |z(u)|^2\,du
 =\frac{|w(0)|^2}{3}s^3+O(s^4),
\]
so physical time is strictly ordered across sufficiently small opposite
punctured sides.  The implemented checker obtains global ordering on its
certified local interval from analyticity, nonidentity of $z$, and
$dt/ds=|z|^2$.

\begin{lemma}[Punctured LC--Newton equivalence]\label{lem:projection}
Let $X(s)$ solve~\eqref{eq:lcfield}, satisfy $C=0$, and have $\rho>0$.
Under $q=Q(z)$, $v=L(z)w/\rho$, and $dt/ds=\rho$, the projected variables
satisfy the planar Newtonian three-body equations.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identities
\[
 L^TL=LL^T=4\rho I,\qquad Lz=2q,
\]
and
\[
 \rho L(w)w-\rho_sL(z)w=-2|w|^2q
\]
reduce the projected relative acceleration to
\[
 q_{tt}=\frac{(-2|w|^2+\rho h)q}{\rho^3}+P.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:constraint} gives
\[
 q_{tt}=-\frac{Mq}{|q|^3}+P.
\]
The equations for $R,U,y,V$ similarly give
$R_t=U$, $R_{tt}=B$, $y_t=V$, and $y_{tt}=Y$; substituting the definitions of
$B,Y,P$ reconstructs all three Newton equations.  The exact symbolic
verification script independently reduces to zero twelve projection and gauge
identities supporting this reduction and the gluing extension below.
\end{proof}

The constraint is essential.  Off $C=0$, the effective singular coefficient
is $-(M+C)q/\rho^3$.  A positive anchor ball centered on a constrained state
would include off-constraint states; this is why the collision certificate
requires zero anchor error rather than a merely small residual.

\section{Certified two-sided passage}

We now state the precise conditional result implemented by the checker.

\begin{theorem}[Validated local planar binary-collision passage]
\label{thm:passage}
Fix positive masses and a pair $(i,j)$.  Let a serialized polynomial
$\bar X$ approximate the lifted LC state on $[s_-,s_+]$, where
$s_-<0<s_+$.  Suppose the checker accepts:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item an LC a posteriori tube centered at $\bar X$, with zero anchor error at
      $s=0$;
\item the exact collision identities~\eqref{eq:anchor} and exact consistency
      of every serialized mass ratio used by the lifted and physical systems;
\item separated-third-body denominators on the entire LC tube;
\item complete projected endpoint enclosures at $s_-$ and $s_+$ with a
      positive lower bound for $\rho$; and
\item weighted ordinary Newtonian tubes containing the respective complete
      projected endpoint enclosures.
\end{enumerate}
Assume the checker's interval operations and interval automatic
differentiation are inclusion isotonic and outward rounded.  Then there is a
unique analytic lifted solution on $[s_-,s_+]$ with an isolated selected-pair
collision at $s=0$.  Its projection on each punctured side is a classical
planar Newtonian three-body solution.  Both branches and the accepted ordinary
tubes continuing them arise from the same lifted IVP, and physical time is
strictly ordered from the left endpoint through collision to the right
endpoint.  The accepted data therefore certify one local generalized
Levi--Civita binary-collision continuation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:tube} gives one unique analytic lifted solution throughout the
LC interval.  Exact anchor arithmetic and
Lemma~\ref{lem:constraint} place this solution on $C=0$ and prove a simple,
isolated zero of $z$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:projection} supplies Newtonian dynamics
where $\rho>0$.  Interval projection of the entire LC endpoint enclosures,
followed by containment in independently validated weighted ordinary tubes,
binds both ordinary branches to that same lifted solution.  Finally,
$dt/ds=|z|^2\geq0$ and analyticity with $z\not\equiv0$ imply a strictly
positive physical-time increment on every nondegenerate subinterval.
\end{proof}

This theorem does not say that arbitrary initial data reach collision.  It is
an a posteriori theorem: supplied data are accepted only after all listed
inequalities and identities have been recomputed.

\section{Checker architecture}

The proof artifact separates construction from verification.  Fast numerical
or formal-series code generates candidate polynomial charts.  The checker
then recomputes the following obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item schema, chart identifiers, dimensions, masses, pairs, intervals, and
      endpoint ordering;
\item direct interval residuals rather than sampled or coefficient-only
      residual surrogates;
\item a full interval enclosure of $DF$ by forward automatic differentiation;
\item outward upper bounds for Jacobian row sums and Gr\"onwall expressions;
\item directed lower bounds for all force denominators and for punctured
      endpoint $\rho$;
\item exact rational collision-anchor and pair-energy identities;
\item exact agreement of the binary floating-point values used for $M$,
      $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $m_k/M$ with their rational values for the
      serialized masses;
\item interval projection of complete endpoint tubes, not only their centers;
      and
\item weighted ordinary tube validation and endpoint containment.
\end{enumerate}

The exact mass-ratio gate is restrictive: many generic floating-point masses
will fail it even when a sound interval enclosure of the ratio would suffice.
It is used here to prevent the implementation from silently proving a theorem
for a nearby mass system.  Replacing it with outward interval coefficients is
a natural extension.

The checker reports separate booleans for collision anchoring, projected
endpoint enclosure, punctured Newtonian projection, and full two-sided
passage.  This separation prevents a weaker endpoint-containment result from
being promoted accidentally to collision continuation.

\section{Reproducible example}

The included example takes

 (m_0,m_1,m_2)=(1,1,1.2),\qquad (i,j)=(0,1),
\]
with collision anchor $z(0)=0$, $w(0)=(1,0)$, LC endpoints
$s_\pm=\pm10^{-2}$, and a separated third body.  A degree-twelve candidate LC
series and degree-twelve ordinary endpoint series are generated through the
public implementation.  Running
\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/certify_planar_binary_collision_passage.py
\end{verbatim}
returns a JSON object in which both of the following Boolean fields are true:
\begin{center}
\texttt{collision\_certified}\qquad
\texttt{passage\_certified}.
\end{center}
In the current fixture, the reported
punctured $\rho$ lower bounds are approximately

The ordinary endpoint charts in this proof-of-concept fixture cover only

\section{Trusted kernel and limitations}

The mathematical conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:passage} is conditional on
sound outward-rounded interval arithmetic; IEEE~1788 is a historical standard
for interval arithmetic, although the 2015 edition is now inactive-reserved
\cite{IEEE1788_2015}, and a general reference is
Moore, Kearfott, and Cloud~\cite{MooreKearfottCloud2009}.  The present kernel comprises the
serialized Python checker, NumPy binary64 operations where explicitly
enclosed, directed \texttt{nextafter} inflation, directed high-precision
decimal square-root, reciprocal-power, and exponential bounds, exact rational
accumulation for sensitive row sums and distance calculations, and interval
forward automatic differentiation.  The witness path has adversarial tests
for the directed elementary bounds.  Nevertheless, this implementation has
not been extracted into or verified by a proof assistant.  In particular,
the exponential enclosure uses \texttt{Decimal.exp} under a directed decimal
context; this runtime behavior is part of the hypothesis and is not here
proved to be a correctly rounded transcendental implementation.  The
executable should therefore not be presented as a foundational proof
independent of its trusted runtime.

The present work is limited in the following ways.
\begin{itemize}
\item It certifies one local example, not a parameter family or an exhaustive
      collision atlas.
\item It is planar and treats only one isolated binary collision.
\item It does not address simultaneous or total collision.
\item It establishes neither global existence nor global atlas termination.
\item Exact representability of certain mass ratios is a conservative
      implementation restriction.
\item An independent implementation audit and comparison with an established
      interval package remain desirable.
\end{itemize}

Accordingly, the scientifically useful claim is narrow: the artifact checks
whether a supplied regularized chart describes one Newtonian solution passing
through one isolated planar binary collision.

\section{Exact anchored gauge gluing and raw gauge-aware entry}

The implementation contains a separate, bounded extension for comparing
already validated LC charts.  Define the deck action
\[
 \Gamma(z,w,h,R,U,y,V,t)=(-z,-w,h,R,U,y,V,t).
\]
Direct substitution gives $F(\Gamma X)=D\Gamma F(X)$.  An atlas vertex in this
extension is one fixed chart, one zero-error tube, and that tube's anchor; a
vertex identifier cannot be rebound to different data.

\begin{proposition}[Exact same-IVP anchored overlap]
Let two accepted zero-error LC tubes use the same positive masses and ordered
pair, with chart intervals $I_u,I_v$ and anchors $a_u,a_v$.  Suppose
\[
 J=(I_u-a_u)\cap(I_v-a_v)
\]
is nondegenerate and exact rational evaluation of all fourteen coordinates
gives
\[
 \bar X_v(a_v)=\Gamma^\sigma\bar X_u(a_u),
 \qquad \sigma\in\mathbb{F}_2,
\]
where the $z$ and $w$ blocks are jointly equal or jointly antipodal and all
other blocks, including physical time, agree.  Then the exact solutions
selected by the tubes satisfy
\[
 X_v(a_v+r)=\Gamma^\sigma X_u(a_u+r),\qquad r\in J.
\]
For a finite connected graph made entirely from such exact comparisons of
fixed vertices, the derived edge bits admit a global vertex-gauge assignment;
their XOR on every cycle is zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The curve $Y(r)=\Gamma^\sigma X_u(a_u+r)$ solves the same autonomous lifted
equations as $X_v(a_v+r)$ by equivariance, and the exact anchor equality gives
the same initial value at $r=0$.  The tube theorem supplies uniqueness, hence
$Y=X_v$ throughout $J$.  Around a graph cycle, composition of the exact edge
relations returns the fixed starting representative.  The checker accepts an
edge only when the identity and antipodal relations are mutually exclusive,
so the action is free on the compared anchor and the composed parity must be
zero.  A root bit propagated along a spanning tree therefore satisfies every
edge equation.
\end{proof}

The overlap checker derives $\sigma$; no parity bit occurs in its serialized
input.  It reports the lifted same-IVP result separately from the stronger
status requiring exact pair-energy constraints and exact mass-ratio arithmetic
for a common punctured Newtonian projection, and separately records whether
$J$ extends to both sides of the anchor.  The raw-evidence aggregate reruns
the vertex tubes and overlap checks, constructs the derived edges, and then
calls an exact $\mathbb{F}_2$ graph checker.  Thus an odd cycle remains a useful
diagnostic for the generic supplied-label kernel, but cannot occur in a fully
accepted free-action same-anchor aggregate.

The implementation also contains a separate raw-evidence entry checker.  Its
source inputs are a direct IVP binding, ordinary a-posteriori tube, and ordinary
chart; it retains those inputs and recomputes the validated source internally.
It accepts neither a prebuilt source result nor a continuation-chain source.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical raw gauge-aware LC entry]
\label{thm:gauge-entry}
Let $S\subset\mathbb R^{12}$ be the ordinary source box reconstructed from
exact rational chart evaluation and the freshly checked ordinary tube radius.
For the ordered selected pair, let the relative-position projection be
$[x_-,x_+]\times[y_-,y_+]$, and suppose it excludes collision.  Apply the
following ordered grammar:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item if $y_-\geq0$, take one closed-upper patch; otherwise, if $y_+\leq0$,
      take one closed-lower patch; otherwise, if $x_->0$, take one right-half
      patch;
\item only if $y_-<0<y_+$ and $x_+<0$, take the closed-upper and closed-lower
      patches and join them by a parity-one edge.
\end{enumerate}
Reject every other rectangle.  Let $B_p\subset\mathbb R^{13}$ be all lifted
patch boxes emitted by the deterministic LC lift, and let the exact graph
checker supply a compatible assignment $a_p\in\mathbb F_2$.  Enumerate that
assignment and its one global complement.  Derive ordinary source time from
the validated binding by
\[
 t_S=t_{\rm init}+s_S-s_{\rm bind}.
\]
If one of the two global assignments satisfies
\[
 \bigl(\Gamma^{a_p}B_p,t_S\bigr)
 \subseteq \mathcal B_{14}(\bar X_*,\varepsilon)
 \qquad\hbox{for every emitted patch }p,
\]
where the right side is one target LC tube's $L^\infty$ initial ball, then
every actual physical source state in $S$ has a constrained selected LC lift
in that target initial ball, and its corresponding lifted solution is enclosed
by the accepted target tube.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The ordered singleton cases cover their accepted source rectangles.  In the
two-patch case,
\[
 P_+=S\cap\{y\geq0\},\qquad P_-=S\cap\{y\leq0\}
\]
cover $S$, and their intersection lies strictly on the negative axis.  There
the principal square roots are $(0,+\sqrt{-x})$ and
$(0,-\sqrt{-x})$; their velocity lifts are jointly antipodal.  Thus the edge
parity is derived as one, and its only compatible assignments are the two
global complements $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$.  The singleton graph likewise has
exactly its principal assignment and global complement.

For each physical $X\in S$, patch coverage supplies a $p$ and the interval
lift supplies a principal constrained representative $\ell_p(X)\in B_p$.
The deck action $\Gamma$ preserves physical projection and the pair-energy
constraint.  The assumed complete box and time containment therefore gives
\[
 \bigl(\Gamma^{a_p}\ell_p(X),t_S\bigr)
 \in\mathcal B_{14}(\bar X_*,\varepsilon).
\]
The target a-posteriori theorem then encloses the exact lifted solution from
that selected initial state.  Since the checker tests every emitted box under
one graph assignment, no source state or patch is discarded and no patch bit
is chosen independently.
\end{proof}

The checker reconstructs geometry and derives parity from raw rational
endpoints; constructor branch booleans, names, reason strings, and parity
metadata are not trusted.  Ambiguous and collision-containing source boxes
reject.  Pair mass and weighted-center ratios are computed in exact rational
arithmetic and converted to outward binary intervals before use in the lifted
boxes.  Every transformed 13-dimensional endpoint and the binding-derived
physical-time coordinate must fit the same target radius; a separate handoff
cap cannot replace this fourteenth-coordinate test.  These close the three
previously identified prerequisites: canonical boundary/parity handling,
outward exact-rational mass coefficients, and clock-correct 14-dimensional
containment.

The legacy ordinary-to-LC checker is separately narrowed to direct validated
results with exact equality of parameter and physical-time widths and zero
binding time gap.  It nevertheless consumes a prebuilt source result and keeps
that inherited trust boundary.  The raw checker in
Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge-entry} is the adversarially replayable route.  Neither
checker result is consumed by a pre-existing continuation or global-atlas
proof-certification path.  This theorem does not certify recentered
continuation chains, arbitrary-data coverage, or a general/global solution.

\section{Directions for further work}

The next proof-engineering stage is raw continuation-chain evidence: retain and
replay every source binding, tube, chart, and ordinary handoff rather than
passing a prebuilt continuation result.  Recentered LC handoffs then require
exact parameter translations and compatible parameter-shift cocycles.  A
stable serialized format and a checker independent of the candidate generator
remain desirable.  Replacing the floating-point interval layer with an audited
arbitrary-precision backend would reduce the trusted base.  Batch generation
could then test whether this theorem yields nontrivial certified families
indexed by masses, collision energy, incoming data, and third-body state.  Such
families could support rigorous studies of local collision scattering maps,
periodic-orbit continuation, and bifurcation.

For practical pre-collision boxes, a parametric Krawczyk treatment of the
Levi--Civita square-root branches may be sharper than the current conservative
interval branch atlas.  In three dimensions, analogous work would require a
careful Kustaanheimo--Stiefel gauge and projection certificate.  These are
extensions of the local certificate architecture; none follows automatically
from Theorem~\ref{thm:passage}.
